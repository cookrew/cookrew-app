Cookrew · R29 · copy review

Three plain rows, one of which isn't plain yet

The four new surfaces of the save-then-popout walk, audited as first-class copy, plus the rail vignette against the two readers it claims to serve.

Velvet (UX Optimizer) · 2026-08-24 · reviewing docs/design/export-ux-demo.html (R29) · third in the series with gate-sheet-review.html and export-walk-review.html

R29 is the right split and the mental model at the end of the walk is the best line in any of these docs: save = keep · publish = sell or share · grant = lend. Three verbs, no protocol. If the product teaches only that, it has taught enough.

Below: one row of the selector fails the owner's bar (and fails it on a rule I wrote), plus four smaller findings and two things dropped in the rewrite.

1WHO GETS THIS — row 2 fails the never-read-a-spec test

Rows 1 and 3 pass cleanly. Row 2 is the one that breaks, and it breaks on the exact rule the vocabulary audit set: "sign in" promises a thing that does not exist. There is no account. The label says ANYONE WHO SIGNS IN and its own sub-line then says with a passkey — so the row contradicts itself in two lines, and a person who has never read a spec reads the label, goes looking for a login, and finds none.

There is a deeper reason to change it. The author is choosing between three audiences — nobody, everybody free, everybody paid. Whether Cookrew asks each installer for a passkey is our implementation detail, not the author's decision, and putting it in the label makes the author think they are choosing an identity policy. Move the mechanism to the sub-line and the choice becomes a clean parallel three.

🔒 JUST MEStays on your shelf. This is where it is now. — unchanged, and "this is where it is now" is a lovely touch
🔑 ANYONE, FREEFree to install. Cookrew asks each person for a passkey first, so you see a count — never strangers' data. — replaces "ANYONE WHO SIGNS IN — FREE"
◈ ANYONE WHO PAYSSet a price. Buyers pay you directly — Cookrew never holds the money and takes nothing. — unchanged; the money line is verbatim from the module
And the reversibility line needs its bound. "Change your answer any time — including back to JUST ME, which unlists it" will be read as I can take it back. Unlisting stops new installs; it does not recall the copies people already have, and it cannot. Say so in the same breath, or the first author who discovers it learns that we let them believe otherwise.
→ "Change your answer any time — including back to JUST ME, which takes it off the marketplace. People who already installed it keep their copy."

2MINE — the tag names ownership, but the state is privacy

Everything on the shelf is mine, published or not. The distinction the dashed border draws is not shared yet, and "MINE" does not say that — after publishing, the chip is still mine, it just also exists elsewhere.

Use JUST ME as the tag. Same words as the selector's first row, one popout away: a chip tagged JUST ME is one whose answer to "who gets this?" is JUST ME. Publishing changes the answer and the tag together, and the popout's MAKE IT JUST ME already speaks this vocabulary — the tag is the only place still speaking the other one. One word, three surfaces, no translation.

3SAVE TO MY PRESETS — keep the length, add the missing reassurance

I tried to shorten it to SAVE AS PRESET and talked myself out of it: naming the destination answers "where did it go?" at the moment of the act, and the shelf beside it is literally called MY PRESETS. The length is worth it. Keep as drawn.

But the reassurance is in your annotation, not in the product. "No sheet, no questions, nothing leaves the machine" is exactly what makes this button safe to press — and it currently lives in the doc, where the author will never see it. A first-timer meeting an unfamiliar SAVE button in a product with a marketplace in it can reasonably fear it publishes something.
→ New string, on the save toast: "Saved to My Presets. Nothing left your machine."
One line, at the one moment the fear exists, and it makes the second save free of hesitation.

5The rail vignette — correct as rationale, wrong as UI, and short one string

Does it survive both readers? As design rationale it is excellent and I would not change a word: "diamonds are ephemeral, a pin is permanent, named, addressable" is the clearest statement of the pin's purpose anyone has written, and "one mark, three duties" is genuinely why the pin earns a third marker class.

But none of it may become UI copy, and one reader has no string at all. "third marker class", "same anchor space", "addressable" are our words. Worse, "one mark, three duties" is a designer's unification: a buyer does not need to know that the author's edition and a caller's fork exist on someone else's rail, and telling them is noise about a stranger's machine.

Each reader needs one sentence, and only the author has one:
  • Author — mkt.export.pin: "A copy was cut here. This is what callers get; your live session carries on above it." ✓ landed.
  • Buyer — no string exists. Their pin means one thing: this is the version you own. Proposed mkt.pin.buyer: "You're running v{n} of {presetName}. Your own work carries on above this." Nothing about editions, nothing about forks.
The vignette claims the mark serves three duties; the product currently has words for one.

6Dropped in the rewrite — naming them, per the review bar

  • The revoke failure strip is gone. My E3 finding is moot by deletion rather than fixed — which means the honest-failure state went with it. "They still have access" is the one string in this surface where a calming tone would be a lie with consequences, and step 6 now draws only the happy path. Put it back, with the second remedy (or un-export the agents to cut it off now) that the previous draft also dropped.
  • The toggle's safety sentence is out of frame. Not a regression — the toggle still exists, the walk just no longer shows it — but my E1 endorsement of its placement is now untested here, and the landed mkt.export.safety (three verbs, including never resumed by anyone else) is still the one to use when it is drawn.
  • E2 is unfixed, carried forward: "one character is wrong" remains a claim the checker cannot support, and the actor is still unnamed — it is buyers who would pay a bad address.
  • Third document without them: the attestation button (I COMPARED THESE · ENROL) and the key-paste step with its six wrong-paste cases, including the private-key refusal. Absent from the gate sheet, the previous walk, and this one. That is no longer a gap in a document; it is a gap in the design.
