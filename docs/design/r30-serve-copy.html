Cookrew · R30 · vocabulary audit + caller copy

Three words, and the one that must never reach a caller

Auditing TEMPLATE · SERVE · SESSION against the no-glossary bar, then the /svc/ page copy and the two owner reassurances that carry the feature.

Velvet (UX Optimizer) · 2026-08-24 · reviewing R30 docs/design/export-ux-demo.html

R30 is the clearest of the three, and the reason is that it stopped describing a protocol and started describing a place. "Their own workspace, in your app, on your version" is a thing a person can picture. Nothing in R28 or R29 was picturable.

The vocabulary survives with one deletion, one confinement and one correction. Then the copy.

1The three words, tested

WordVerdictWhy
TEMPLATEsurvives as-is Universal, and a stranger's guess — "a starting point you make copies from" — is exactly right. The pin gloss is for us; nobody needs it to use the button.
SERVEsurvives, taught by its own success state "Serve" alone is server jargon and a stranger would not bet on its meaning. But the demo already teaches it in the right place: press START SERVING and the next thing you read is "Research Crew is taking calls." A term introduced by the sentence beside it is a legitimate way to introduce a term. Keep SERVE; make every surface carry "takes calls" alongside it, not only the LIVE state.
SESSIONowner-side only — never show it to a caller Two problems. A stranger's "session" is a login: ephemeral, weightless. This one is a workspace with a folder that parks for hours and survives your V2 — their model will be far too small. And it collides with our own code, where "session" is a transcript (claudeSessionId, sessionLineage). Owner-side it is fine as a plural for the table — "who's using it right now". Caller-side it must never appear: a caller has a workspace, not a session, and mostly they need no noun at all.
Delete the fourth word. The demo says "publish it as a SERVICE" and the route is /svc/. That is a fourth term the bar does not budget for, and it is the vaguest of the four — "service" is what every piece of software calls itself. The author serves a template; there is no separate object called a service. Keep /svc/ as a URL (nobody reads a path as vocabulary) and strike "service" from prose.

2A privacy promise R30 breaks, and it is mine

The demo says "You see who's on, never their data" — and the sessions table shows ana@studio by name. Under R30 the author does see caller identities, and that is defensible: they are hosting real work on their machine and END is their panic button, which they cannot aim at a person they cannot see.

But my landed string says the opposite. mkt.auth.why = "Authors can see how many people installed a preset, never who you are." That was true for a download. It is false for a session, and it is shown to the caller at the moment they identify themselves.

Fix, and it must be the caller-facing one: a new string for services that tells the truth before they commit, not after.
mkt.svc.privacy — "{author} can see that you're here and can end your workspace. They can't see inside it."
That last clause is the part worth having, and it needs Atlas to confirm it: the author hosts the workspace, so "can't see inside it" is a claim about what the product surfaces, not about what is technically reachable. If the author can read a caller's transcript, this sentence must say so instead. I will not ship a privacy promise I cannot stand behind.

3Still unfixed from R29, second time of asking

"ANYONE WHO SIGNS IN — FREE" is unchanged. There is no account and no sign-in; the sub-line still says "passkey" one line below the label that contradicts it. The fix is the same as last time and costs one word: ANYONE, FREE, with the passkey fact in the sub-line. Raising it again because it is the row a stranger meets first, and because a label that survives two reviews becomes the product.

mkt.svc.ended is the hardest string on the page and I have written it as bluntly as I can. A stranger paid money, did work, and someone else's button deleted it. Softening that is the temptation and it would be the lie: "your files were in the workspace and are gone with it" is the sentence they need, and if the sandbox is retained after END then it must say that instead. Atlas: which is it? The copy is one clause different depending on the answer, and the caller's next action — panic, or not — depends entirely on which we ship.

5The two owner reassurances

Your workspace is never touched

The landed mkt.export.safety is about conversations, which was the R28/R29 object. R30's object is a workspace, so this is a new string rather than an edit — and the strongest version does not stop at "safe", it says you can carry on, because the fear is not really about damage, it is about having to be careful.

mkt.serve.safety Callers never touch your workspace. Each one gets a fresh copy of the template you pinned, in its own folder. Keep working exactly as you did before — they can't see it, and nothing you do now reaches them.

That final clause is doing the second half of the job: it tells the author their ongoing work is invisible to callers, which is the question they ask on day two. The version pin is what makes it true, so the sentence and the architecture agree by construction.

END stops a session mid-call

Same shape as revoke, and the same rules: no confirm on the safe direction is wrong here — END destroys a caller's work, so it is the one control in the serving surface that must confirm, and the confirm carries the consequence rather than asking "are you sure".

mkt.serve.end.confirm.titleEnd {caller}'s workspace?
mkt.serve.end.confirm.body Anything it's doing right now stops, and their folder is removed. They'll be told you ended it. Your own workspace and template are untouched.
mkt.serve.end.confirm.actionEND IT
mkt.serve.end.done{caller}'s workspace ended.
mkt.serve.end.error Couldn't end {caller}'s workspace — it's still running and they can still work. Try again, or stop serving to end every workspace at once.
mkt.serve.stop.confirm Stop serving Research Crew? {n} workspaces end now, including any mid-call. The template stays on your shelf.
Three rules carried into these, none of them new: the failure leads with the frightening half ("they can still work") because a soft failure on a kill switch is where a calming tone becomes a lie; the failure offers the second remedy (stop serving) because the first one just failed; and the confirm names what survives ("your own workspace and template are untouched"), because an author reaching for END at speed needs to know the blast radius stops at the caller.

6What I could not answer without Atlas

  • Does the author see inside a caller's workspace? mkt.svc.privacy claims not. If they can read a caller's transcript, that string must say so — it is shown before payment, so it is the most consequential sentence on the page.
  • Does the sandbox survive END? mkt.svc.ended currently says the files are gone. One clause changes if they are retained, and the caller's reaction changes with it.
  • Does a parked workspace keep its files? The table shows "parked 2h" and parking is described as costing nothing. If parking is lossless, callers should be told — it is the difference between "come back tomorrow" and "finish tonight or lose it".
