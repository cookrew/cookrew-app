Three judgements, and one strip that contradicts itself

Reviewing docs/design/export-ux-demo.html against grant-sheet-flows.html, marketplace-copy-deck.html and shared/marketplace-copy.ts.

Velvet (UX Optimizer) · 2026-08-24 · companion to gate-sheet-review.html

Verdict: the walk is right and two of its ideas are better than mine. "The refusal happens here, to the person who can fix it" is a principle I did not have and am adopting. "Author and buyer read one truth" — the same scrub list on both sides of the wire — is the strongest single idea in any of these decks, because it makes the author's honesty and the buyer's safety the same artifact rather than two documents that can drift.

Four findings. One is a contradiction in the surroundings you asked me to check (E3).

1E1 — the toggle's safety sentence: placement right, words weaker

Placement: endorse, and it is better than my deck had it. Beside the switch, always visible, before the flip — the fear is what stops the action, so the answer must precede it. And "the entry point, not a settings checkbox: flipping it reveals the two doors" is a better model of what that control is than anything I wrote.

SentenceFears answered
Draft"Exporting never shares your conversations. Calls and copies run against a fork — your original session is never touched." exfiltration · mutation
Landed
mkt.export.safety		 "Callers get a copy. Your original conversation is never touched, never sent, and never resumed by anyone else." mutation · exfiltration · impersonation

Use the landed string. The three verbs are not decoration — they answer three different questions, and the draft drops the third. "Never resumed by anyone else" is the agent-specific fear: not can they read my conversation but can someone pick it up and carry on as me. For an exported agent that is the sharpest worry of the three, and it is the one a paraphrase loses first because it sounds redundant until you notice it is a different verb.

2E2 — the payout-checksum refusal: blocking is right, two copy fixes

The behaviour is correct and the principle is a keeper. Refusing at publish rather than at the buyer's 402 puts the failure in front of the only person who can fix it, and "PUBLISH — FIX PAYOUT FIRST" says why instead of greying out mutely, which is my own never-a-silently- dead-control rule applied better than I applied it.

Fix 1 — "one character is wrong" is a claim the checker cannot support. A failed checksum tells you the address is invalid. It does not tell you how many characters are wrong, or which; it could be two, or a truncation, or a paste from the wrong wallet entirely. This is the same class of defect as my own seats string: copy asserting a fact the code does not have. It will read as precise and be wrong, and an author who "fixes one character" and fails again has been misled by us.
→ "This address doesn't verify. Check it against your wallet — a single wrong character is enough to break it."

Fix 2 — name the actor. "Money sent here is gone" is true but reads as a warning about the author's own money. The risk is that buyers pay this address. That is both scarier and more accurate, and it explains why we block rather than warn.
→ "Your buyers would pay this address, and money sent to a bad address is not recoverable."

3E3 — the revoke failure strip: rule carried, surroundings contradict it

The strip itself is right and improved: "They still have access — revoke didn't complete." leads with the frightening half, and naming the specific pair ("ana@studio can still call Tinker") is better than my generic {name}. That part I would ship as drawn.

But you asked me to check the surroundings, and they undo it twice.

(a) The second remedy is missing. The landed string is "Couldn't revoke {name} — they still have access. Try again, or un-export the agents to cut it off now." The draft keeps "Retry now" and drops the second clause. That clause is the whole point of the asymmetry: a failed revoke is exactly when you want the panic control, and un-export is the one-switch kill that still works when per-caller revoke is the thing failing. Offering only "retry" against a mechanism that just failed is offering the author the button that did not work.

(b) The explainer beneath it contradicts the strip. Directly under the failure sits "Revoke stops calls already running. Their conversations were forks — your originals were never touched." Read in place, that says revoke didn't complete and then revoke stops calls already running — a description of success sitting under a message saying it did not happen. Both sentences are true in general and the pair is misleading exactly where it is drawn. An author skimming reads the reassuring one and walks away believing the line is cut.
→ That explainer belongs on the revoke control at rest, or on success. Never under a failure. Under a failure, the only thing that may appear is what is still true right now — and right now the caller still has access.

4E4 — the attestation and the key-paste are both absent

Step 5 shows the six words and "Same six words on both screens = same key. Different words = STOP" — good, and the STOP line is better than my original. But two things from the grant deck are missing from the author's own walk:

  • The attestation button. I COMPARED THESE · ENROL does not appear; the panel ends at "ENROL NEW CALLER". The label is the mechanism — it collects a statement rather than a reflex — so a walk that shows the words without the claim shows the theatre without the guarantee.
  • The key ever arriving. Six words are derived from a key, and no step pastes one. The wrong-paste taxonomy — six cases, including the private-key refusal that must clear the field and tell the author to rotate — has no home in either this walk or the gate sheet. It is the same gap in both documents, which is how a gap becomes permanent.

  • "Your conversations are not in this list because they are never in the package." New, mine to steal, and the best sentence in the walk — it answers the fear by pointing at an absence the author can see for themselves, which beats any promise.
