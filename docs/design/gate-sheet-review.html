The Gate Sheet, audited against the two decks it supersedes

Verdict, seven findings, and answers to the three questions. Reviewing docs/design/gate-sheet-unified.html against wallet-sheet-flows.html, grant-sheet-flows.html and shared/marketplace-copy.ts.

Velvet (UX Optimizer) · 2026-08-24 · Fresco builds after this pass

Verdict: adopt the frame. "The sheet is a picture of authorize()" is a better organising idea than either deck I wrote — it makes UI drift from the protocol structurally hard rather than merely policed, and progress-as-subtraction is the right shape for a flow whose whole job is to get out of the way. Door A and Door B as one sheet with different steps lit is correct.

Seven findings below. One is structural and blocks the build (F1); one is a status correction on the verbatim claim (F2); one is a ruling regression (F3); the rest are missing states.

1F1 — the review step has no home, and it is the safety surface

BLOCKING

The sheet mirrors authorize(), which ends at serve. But installing has a step the gate does not contain: the human reading what they are about to run — the scrub report, the shell commands verbatim, the workdir, the worktree choice. Panel B goes straight from identify to "Yours. Placing on your canvas…".

That step is the security surface of the entire marketplace, and after R28 it lives in neither deck: the wallet deck never had it, the grant deck is a different surface, and the unified sheet skips it because authorize() has no opinion about it.

Fix: add a REVIEW tick to the rail, after serve, before the pin. The rail is the user's journey, not the gate's function — and this is the one place the mirror must not be literal. Everything else about the metaphor survives; the sheet simply has one step the protocol does not, which is honest, because the protocol does not decide whether you run something, you do.

R2 is unaffected: a chip click for an owned, unchanged preset still places immediately. Review is shown once per preset+version, which is the first pass through this rail.

3F3 — R15 regression: balance_empty loses a tense

The draft reads "Your call credit ran out. Top up from the chip — your conversation is safe and resumes on the same fork." That keeps the future half and drops the past half. R15 requires both, and each is backed by a different ruling: the past ("Its last answer completed — nothing was lost") is true by R12, drawdown at accept with no interrupted turn; the future ("topping up resumes the same conversation") is true by R13, the session stays resident. A buyer at a dry meter is asking both questions — did I lose the answer I was waiting for and can I carry on — and the draft answers only the second.

F3b: the receipt line has the mirror-image problem. Moving it to TERMS is a good idea; removing it from success is not. Before paying it is an argument; after paying it is what you own, and the instant of ownership is the only moment someone is thinking about what their money bought. Both places.

4F4 — the enrol button's actor changed underneath it (answers Q3)

Panel C shows the caller's side of first contact: "Prove it's you — read these to @drej." My grant deck's sheet is the owner's side: the owner pastes a key, compares the words, and enrols. The fingerprint is symmetric, so showing the caller's side is legitimate and the panel is otherwise excellent — "If @drej reads different words, STOP" is better than anything in my deck.

But the verb no longer matches the act. I COMPARED THESE · ENROL is an owner's attestation: I compared, and I am enrolling this key. A caller pressing it enrols nobody — they are the one being enrolled. The attestation half still works (it collects a statement, which is what you asked me to check); the action half is now describing someone else's action.

Fix, caller side: I READ THESE ALOUD · CONNECT. Same mechanism — the label states the claim the click makes, and someone who did not read them aloud must knowingly assert something false. Owner side keeps I COMPARED THESE · ENROL verbatim. Two sides, two verbs, one comparison.

6F6 — a non-finding, with the guard that keeps it one

I checked "Your grant covers Forge and Magpie" against my Q3 privacy merge and it passes, for a reason subtle enough to write down: it discloses only what the caller already holds, and says nothing new about Tinker. The single-string rule exists to stop a prober distinguishing not granted from does not exist, and this sentence does not.

The guard: the first clause — "This line doesn't reach {agent}" — must never vary by cause. The moment a variant says "not exported to you" or "no such agent", the merge is gone. Bucket first, elaborate second, and only ever with facts the caller already owns.

7Your three questions

Q1 — finished 401 collapsing to a one-line green band: right

Identity does not deserve more weight as a step; it deserves its weight while it is the step, and progress-as-subtraction is the correct instinct — a sheet that grows as you succeed is a sheet that punishes success. The collapsed band already keeps the one fact that is not self-evident later ("never a password"), which is the right thing to preserve.
One constraint: the collapsed band must not be tappable. A finished step is a receipt; the moment it can re-open the ceremony it is a form again, and the subtraction is cosmetic.

Q2 — the dashed skip-tick: honest, keep it, with a bound

Dashing is right for a step the gate could have demanded and did not — a free install skipping pay. That is information: it tells the buyer this sheet has a payment step and that theirs was free, which is worth knowing the first time they meet a priced one.
The bound: dash a step this door contains; omit a step it does not. A Door B line with no pricing at all should not carry a ghost pay tick — that is noise, and it invites the buyer to wonder what they avoided. Rule of thumb: dashed means not this time, absent means not here.

Q3 — I COMPARED THESE · ENROL in its new surroundings

The surroundings collect a statement, yes — the six words are large, the comparison is named as out-of-band, and "If @drej reads different words, STOP" does more work than my original. But the actor changed and the verb did not follow it. See F4: caller side becomes I READ THESE ALOUD · CONNECT, owner side keeps the original word for word.
