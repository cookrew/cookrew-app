Cookrew · marketplace · vocabulary & gaps audit

Where does a user learn what any of this means?

Every surface that speaks to a buyer or an author, audited as ONE vocabulary: the canonical word for each protocol state, where each surface deviates from it, and the ten sentences the product is currently missing at its ten moments of doubt.

Velvet (UX Optimizer) · 2026-08-22 · surfaces read: registry/src/install-copy.ts · install-page.ts · src/shared/marketplace-copy.ts · preset-manifest.ts · the review-sheet payload · the three design decks

Doc annotation (R14). 401, 402, 403, seat_limit and friends appear here as design labels. A buyer never sees any of them — which is exactly why this audit exists: the numbers are not the vocabulary, the words are, and nobody has been auditing the words as a set.

0The finding, before the detail

The vocabulary is designed and largely unshipped. Three decks specify well over a hundred user-facing strings; marketplace-copy.ts holds 17. Every string that exists is correct and lifted verbatim — the discipline is real — but the surfaces a confused user actually reaches are mostly silent, and the two that do speak use three different words for the same state.

■ 17 landed   ■ ~93 specified, not yet in any module. The gap is not a criticism of the build order — rotation and the two fallbacks were the right first strings. It is the answer to the Commander's question: today, a user learns what these mean mostly by not learning.

1The canonical vocabulary

One word per state, everywhere, forever. Where a surface uses a different one, that is a defect row in §2 — not a synonym.

Protocol stateCANONICAL user wordNever sayWhy this word
401 identity"Prove it's you" · the act is a passkey sign in · log in · account · unlock · authenticate There is no account and no password. "Sign in" promises a thing that does not exist and teaches the buyer to look for a forgotten one.
402 payment"Buy" / "Pay {amount} {asset}" unlock · upgrade · subscribe · checkout Name the transaction, not a metaphor. "Unlock" hides whether money moves.
403 entitlement"Your licence doesn't cover this" + the specific reason forbidden · denied · not authorised · access denied 403 is six different situations; a single word for all of them is what produced the wrong-remedy bug.
scope"Cookrew couldn't get permission for that" scope · token · credential mismatch It is our defect, not the buyer's decision. Blame no one and offer no purchase.
balance_empty"Out of credit" · the unit is a call turn · quota · credits exhausted Internals charge per turn; the buyer is calling a teammate. One word, theirs.

key rotation"{author} changed signing keys" security alert · key compromise Usually ordinary. Alarm language spends trust we need for the real event.
exportable"can be called by" / "exported" shared · published · public "Shared" reads as read-only. This is execution by someone else.

3The money story, in one breath

Asked whether an author can say where the price is set, where the money lands, and what the registry takes: the architecture answers all three cleanly and no surface says any of it. The facts live in terms.ts, payment.ts and facilitator.ts — code comments, read by us.

QuestionTrue answerSaid anywhere?
Where is the price set?By the author, in the manifest they sign.no
Where does the money land?The author's address, directly. Chain settlement.no
What does the registry take?Nothing. It never holds funds at all.no
What did I buy — once, or per use?model: 'one-time' | 'per-call' the field exists; the buy sheet shows the amount, and the deck's cadence line is unshipped
Per seat?unmodelledno — and my copy claims otherwise (D3)
Updates included?unmodelledno
Recommendation. Two optional manifest fields — seats?: number and updatesIncluded?: 'this-version' | 'all-future' — close the buyer's two biggest unknowns and cost the protocol almost nothing, because both are already expressible as 403 reasons (seat_limit, version_gate) that the gate can enforce. Right now the product can refuse a buyer for exceeding a limit it never told them about. That asymmetry — enforced but unstated — is the actual bug, and it is a protocol gap rather than a copy one.

4The export story — the fear nobody answers

Exporting is safe by construction: the scrubber strips secrets and paths, the manifest is signed, a call forks the transcript, and a version pin marks the cut. The original session is never mutated — version-pin.ts opens with exactly that sentence.

🫥
It is a code comment. It has never been said to an author. "If I export this agent, do strangers get my conversation?" is the number-one reason not to export, we solved it in the architecture, and there is no surface where an author can read that we solved it. A guarantee the user cannot see does not change their behaviour — it only makes us feel safe on their behalf.

6The ten missing sentences

One per moment of doubt. Each is a sentence the product should contain and does not; each is placed, not floating.

  1. What a preset is — install page, exists as mkt.install.lede and is the one good answer already written. Keep it and reuse it in-app, where the market card currently assumes you know.
  2. What the identity step is — replaces D1: "This preset asks who you are before it downloads. One passkey gesture in Cookrew — no password, no account to create."
  3. Why identity at all — passkey sheet, one line under the action: "Authors can see how many people installed a preset, never who you are."
  4. Where the money goes — buy sheet, beside the price: "Paid directly to {author}. Cookrew never holds your money and takes nothing."
  5. What you are buying — buy sheet: "One-time — yours to place as often as you like." / "Per call — you top up a balance and each call draws from it." Two strings, chosen by pricing.model.
  6. What happens to my session if I export — the export/exportable toggle, and the most important sentence in this document: "Callers get a copy. Your original conversation is never touched, never sent, and never resumed by anyone else."
  7. What leaves the machine — export confirm: "Cookrew strips secrets and folder paths before anything is published, and shows you the report first."
  8. Who can call this agent right now — the agent row, at rest, not in a panel: "{n} callers" or "Nobody can call this". Access should be legible without opening anything, because the question is asked in a glance.
  9. What a version pin is — the rail, on first pin: "A copy was cut here. This is what buyers get; your live session carries on above it."
  10. What the transparency log proves — and does not — beside the log link: "A public record of every publish. It shows if a past entry was changed; it can't prove an author is honest." (R23 bounds this; the honest version is the only one we may ship.)

7What I would fix first

  1. Sentence 6 — the export fear. One line, unblocks the feature's entire premise, costs nothing, and is already true.
  2. D1 — "account" on the install page. It is wrong on the surface most strangers see, and it is a two-word fix.
  3. D3 / §3 — either model seats and updates, or stop implying them. Enforcing a limit we never disclosed is the one item here with a real support cost.
  4. Sentence 4 — "Cookrew takes nothing" is the most persuasive true sentence the product has, and it is not written down anywhere a person can read it.
