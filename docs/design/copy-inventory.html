2The two identity vocabularies, and the wall

Accounts at the public door · six words between humans who know each other. MKT_AUTH and MKT_SVC speak accounts — a stranger at /svc/, the install page, any 401, where the parties have never met and an account is what a registry can check. MKT_ENROL speaks six words — a LAN, owner to caller, compared out loud over a channel we do not control, where no registry is involved and none could help.

They are kept apart by a function, not a habit. identityVocabularyLeaks() fails on any string reaching for both, and two further tests check each side. The failure it prevents is not untidiness: a ceremony that mentions accounts sends a person looking for a registry to vouch for a key no registry has ever seen, and the comparison — the only thing making enrolment safe — starts to feel like a formality someone else handled.

3Decisions inside the strings, not beside them

  • JUST ME, not MINE. Everything on the shelf is mine, published or not; the state being drawn is NOT SHARED. JUST ME is also the serve sheet's first row, so the tag and the answer are one word.
  • The reversibility bound. "Change it back any time" reads as a recall. Stopping stops NEW callers; anyone already working keeps going until you end them — and the string says so.
  • Parked costs you nothing. Without it an owner ends sessions to save money they were never spending.
  • END confirms. The only control in the serving surface that must, because it destroys someone else's work — and the confirm names what survives, so an author reaching for it at speed knows the blast radius stops at the caller.
  • The reassurance says carry on, not safe. The author's fear is not damage; it is having to be careful in their own workspace.
  • No "session" caller-side; no "service" in prose. Both held by tests.

4Two strings shipped flagged, not confident

mkt.svc.privacy — "They can't see inside it" is a claim about what the product surfaces, not about what is reachable on a machine the author owns. It appears before payment, which makes it the most consequential sentence on the page. If an author can read a caller's transcript, it has to say so instead.

mkt.svc.ended — says a caller's files are gone with the workspace. If the sandbox survives END, that clause is wrong, and the caller's next action — panic, or not — changes with it. Atlas owns both facts; each is a one-clause edit once answered.
