Registry M2 — payments, as built

Forge · 2026-08-23 · feat/registry-m2, four slices on top of dev. This is the note for what EXISTS, written after building it — the companion to docs/design/m2-seam.html, which was written before and predicted most of it. Where the two disagree, this one is right.

A1 · A2 · A3 · A4 greenspec §2 · §4R5 · R11 · R12 · R14 · R24 · R26

M1's design note promised that payment would be one added variant on one function. It held: authorize.ts gained one branch and Verdict gained one member. No route signature moved, no header plumbing changed, and the client's recovery strategy is still "retry the same idempotent GET".

1The ladder, as it now stands

exists? → public? → identity? → scope? → ENTITLED? → PRICED? → serve
                                              ↑            ↑
                                         A3 receipts   A1/A2 402

Two placements carry almost all of the behaviour, and both were things the seam note called and the build confirmed.

PlacementWhy it is thereWhat breaks if it moves
Entitlement ABOVE price A buyer who already owns something gets an ordinary 200 and never reaches payment. Every download re-runs the 402 handshake. Opening something you bought last week costs a wallet gesture — "a buyer must not sign per fetch" becomes politeness rather than structure.
Price LAST, before serve By then we know the preset exists, who is asking, and that their credential covers a download. Asking for money before knowing whether we would have served the bytes at all.
402 AFTER scope (R26) A wrong-scope token is a statement about the credential, not about money. Offering to sell somebody a fix for a problem payment cannot fix.

ONLY A GET IS A PURCHASE — an allow-list, not an exclusion list

GET and HEAD share one authorize(), so a priced preset made R3's dock-open update check answer 402 — and R24 forbids a background check raising a sheet at all. Opening the dock would have demanded payment.

Written as method !== 'HEAD' the fix passed its first gate and still answered 402 to OPTIONS, POST and to a request with no method at all. An exclusion list makes every method somebody adds later a purchase by default. It is now method === 'GET', and the gate that found it lives on the decision function rather than the route — because the failure mode is a second caller, and an HTTP-only test would keep passing while a background check quietly asked for money.

2Where the money goes

Buyer → author. The registry is never in the path. Ruled 2026-08-22 after the spec was found to contradict itself: §4 put payTo in the terms while §12 placed "author payouts" in M3. The deciding argument was that custody is a one-way door — a registry that has never held a buyer's money cannot lose it, cannot be asked to refund it, and does not become a money transmitter.

So M2 needed one fact M1 never had: a payout address per publishing identity, keyed to the IDENTITY and not the author key, because R20 made keys rotatable and a payout binding that went stale exactly when a key rotated would send money somewhere nobody is watching. It is an address book, not a ledger; the moment it grows a balance column somebody has taken custody by accident.

A price needs a payee, enforced at PUBLISH

A priced manifest from an identity with no payout address is refused with payout_missing before any bytes are stored. That puts the failure in front of the AUTHOR, mid-ceremony, one field from fixed — rather than in front of a buyer who cannot fix it and would meet it as a preset that simply never sells.

3The terms, on the wire

HTTP/1.1 402 Payment Required

{
  "terms": {
    "amount": "12.00",        // decimal string; money is never a float
    "asset":  "USDC",
    "chain":  "base",         // named SEPARATELY from the amount (deck §7)
    "payTo":  "0x…",          // the AUTHOR's address
    "nonce":  "…",            // single-use, bound to (identity, preset)
    "expiry": 1766000000000
  },
  "reason": "expired",        // absent on the first ask — see §4
  "retryable": false
}

chain is in the terms, and it was nearly not

It was missing from the field list I was given. A transfer to the correct address on the WRONG CHAIN is money gone with a successful receipt — every party's local view says success — which is the worst failure shape in the feature. The facilitator is handed it and checks it.

4Four reasons, and why exactly four

The rule, ruled 2026-08-22 and worth restating because it decides both directions: distinctness is required where the caller's next action differs, and forbidden where it would let a stranger enumerate. These four are all next-action-distinct and leak nothing, so they stay apart. (The same rule keeps Atlas's ceremony failures indistinguishable, because his are next-action-identical and would reveal whether an identity exists.)

ReasonWhat happenedBuyer's next moveFresh quote?
invalidNot a payment for this preset — malformed, unknown nonce, or refused.Stop; check your wallet.Yes
expiredThe quote stood and the buyer took longer.Re-price and pay.Yes
replayedThis proof already bought something.Nothing — it is already owned.No
unverifiableWE could not check. The facilitator would not answer.Try again; yours may be fine.No

The fourth reason changed who gets a fresh quote

A new nonce reads as "pay again". On unverifiable the money may already have moved and we simply cannot see it; on replayed it certainly has. Minting an offer for either is an invitation to buy one preset twice — so both echo the terms the buyer already holds.

retryable rides as a boolean, not only as a token. The M1 forward-compat rule renders an unknown reason as a sentence, but retryability cannot be guessed from a string a client has never seen — and guessing wrong is exactly the lie the reason exists to prevent. A client that has never heard of unverifiable still knows to retry.

The first ask carries terms and no reason. Not having paid yet is not a thing that went wrong, and a reason there would make the opening move read as a failure. The four are also disjoint from the 403 vocabulary, asserted — both ride in a reason field on the same gate, and disjointness means even a client that ignored the status could not mistake a payment failure for an entitlement refusal.

C16 caught a live collapse, and the story is the argument for the gate

replayed would have collapsed into invalid if a spent nonce were deleted rather than remembered — a replay would read as "we never issued that". That one was designed against.

expired actually did collapse. The price step quoted unconditionally, so a request carrying a proof still minted a fresh offer — and the sweep that ran with it deleted the very nonce being asked about. A buyer who was merely slow was told their payment was invalid. Two root causes, both fixed: a lapsed quote is retained AS lapsed, and price facts are now separate from the act of minting, so a nonce is created only when an offer is genuinely made.

5Receipts, and money that reconciles

Keyed by lineage, not preset id. An id is the content address of one version, so a receipt against it would stop covering the preset the moment its author shipped an update — buying v2 would not entitle v3 and mkt.pay.buys's "v{version} and later" would be false. The earliest purchase sets the floor.

Not in the transparency log. The log's guarantee is about what was served and who signed it. Buyer identities in a public append-only file is a privacy leak with no matching benefit, and it would complicate a log that already carries a carefully narrow claim. Receipts are private, append-only, and a truncated tail stops the read rather than being skipped — records after a gap are purchases we cannot be sure we wrote. They are written BEFORE the bytes are served, so a crash between the two costs a retry that finds the buyer entitled, never a payment with nothing to show for it.

RULE: money is integer cents, never a float

Ten 0.10 credits total exactly 1.00. In binary floating point they do not, and the difference is between a balance that reconciles and one that drifts. Amounts cross the wire as decimal strings and are summed as integers throughout. This is a rule for every surface that touches money, not an implementation detail of this store.

Balance carries both units (deck §7): $0.30 USDC · ~4 calls left. Money alone makes the buyer divide to learn how many answers they have; calls alone hides what a top-up costs. The call count floors — "one left" when there isn't is precisely how balance_empty becomes the mid-conversation surprise R5 forbids.

R5 holds, and is asserted rather than described

The balance is deliberately NOT consulted by the gate. Owning credit entitles the download; whether a given CALL may run is the meter's question, answered on the call path as 200 or 403 and never as a payment demand over a conversation. A dry meter still downloads what you own.

6Idempotency, and what survives a restart

FactWhere it livesSurvives a reboot
What has been boughtreceipts (receipts.jsonl)yes
What a quote was forquotes (quotes.jsonl)yes
Where an author is paidpayouts.jsonyes
Whether a nonce was "spent"nowhere — it is derived from the receipt (below)

One record of one fact

The in-memory spent set is gone rather than kept alongside the receipt. Two records of the same fact can disagree, and the dangerous direction is memory saying spent while no receipt exists: a buyer whose money moved would be told replayed while owning nothing.

So the receipt is the only thing asked, and it fails safe. No receipt means not spent, so a retry can settle again — the proof names the same transaction, and re-verifying a transaction moves no money a second time.

Quotes are durable, and that case was not on the plan. A restart used to destroy the record that a quote was ever issued. A buyer who took one, moved real money against it, and came back to a registry that had bounced would meet invalid — we would tell somebody who had just paid that their payment was not a payment. Same lie as reporting our outage as their fault, by a different road, and worse because it accuses rather than admits. A quote is a promise with a price on it, so it is written down. It still lapses on time: durability is not immortality.

7The facilitator boundary

The registry does two things with money and neither involves touching it: ask for terms, ask whether a proof settled. It is an injected interface, so no test reaches a chain, no key exists and no network is required — which matters beyond hygiene, because the cases that need testing (expired, replayed, refused, unreachable) are exactly the ones a real chain makes hard to produce on demand.

What a compromised facilitator costs, stated as precisely as the log's limit: it can mint entitlements nobody paid for. That is a loss to AUTHORS and it is real. What it cannot do is alter a preset — the client verifies the author's signature and hashes itself (A5) — or forge attribution, which needs a countersigned ceremony it has no part in. The money component's blast radius is bounded to money.

The dev facilitator is its own file under its own name, wired only by the dev entry point. It refuses a refuse- tx and reports an outage for an unreachable- one, so every failure is constructible against the real binary rather than only against a double — a case reachable only by mocking is a case nobody has seen the server answer.

8What M2 does NOT do

My own list, so it is not discovered in review. Nothing here is a surprise; several are deliberate.

Not doneWhere it belongsRisk if forgotten
No real facilitator. The interface is real; the only implementation is the dev stand-in, which reaches no chain and verifies no transfer. Deployment HIGH — this is the difference between "payments work" and "the payment SHAPE works". Nothing in M2 has ever moved money.
No EIP-55 checksum on payout addresses. Format-checked only; keccak256 is not in node:crypto. Before a public network MEDIUM — a mistyped-but-well-formed address sends an author's money nowhere recoverable. Mitigated only by the address arriving at publish, from the author, in a ceremony they are present for.
The call path does not exist. A3 lands the prepaid balance and its reader; nothing meters or draws down, because there is nothing to meter. §9 remote call Low — deliberate. DRAWDOWN_CONTRACT and isCallPathAnswer pin the shape so it cannot drift before then.
No top-up route. A per-call preset can be bought once; there is no way to add credit afterwards. M2 follow-up or M3 MEDIUM — R5 names top-up as a first-class surface and Velvet has written its copy. A balance that can only ever go down is a half-feature.
Single process assumed. Idempotency rests on the decision function being synchronous; two registry processes over one data directory would race. Whenever it is deployed more than once MEDIUM — silent when wrong. Two receipts for one payment, or two payments accepted for one nonce.
No price change story. Pricing is inside the signed manifest, so changing a price needs a new version. Nobody has ruled whether that is intended. Product Low — but it will surprise the first author who tries a sale.
Refund and revocation are untouched. refunded and revoked are in the 403 vocabulary with copy written and no machinery. M3, per §12 Low — explicitly phased.
The install page says nothing about price. A shared link to a paid preset does not mention that it costs money. Velvet — copy slot needed MEDIUM — surprises the buyer at the worst moment. Flagged at A4 of M1 and still open.
No client half. No wallet sheet, no 402 handling in the app, no mkt.pay.* wired. Velvet / Fresco Low — by design; the server half was the assignment.
Receipts have no retention or export. They accumulate forever and there is no way for a buyer to see or carry them. M3 Low now, awkward later.
